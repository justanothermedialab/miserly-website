A register you type into

Miserly is for people who keep their own accounts. You type each transaction into a register, the way you would in a spreadsheet, and click any field to change it. Savings, cards, deposits, funds, property and loans all go in, and the dashboard adds them up into one net worth.

Investments at what they're worth

Most expense trackers only know what you paid in. In Miserly you also type what a holding is worth now, from your fund statement, your PPF passbook or your EPF balance.

Questions

01/Where is my data kept?

In one file, miserly.db. With the Windows app it sits in the data folder next to Miserly.exe; with Docker, on your own server. Miserly sends nothing anywhere: no account with us, no analytics, no tracking.

02/Do I connect my bank?

No. Miserly never asks for bank logins and never reads your SMS, email or statements. You type each transaction in, and what each investment is worth when you check it.

03/What does it cost?

Nothing. Miserly is free and open source under the AGPL-3.0. If it's useful to you, you can sponsor it on GitHub; the app never asks.

04/Can my family use one install?

Yes, on a home server with Docker. Each person has their own login, and their accounts stay private until they share one. A shared account counts in each holder's net worth. How sharing works

05/How do I back up?

Close Miserly and copy miserly.db to another drive, a USB stick or a cloud folder. To restore, copy it back. Miserly also saves a copy of the file before an update changes it. Your data, in the guide

06/I use Actual Budget. Can I bring my data?

Yes. Export your data from Actual's Settings, then choose the file in Miserly's Settings, Import. Accounts, transactions, categories, payees and transfers come across; budgets, rules and schedules don't, since Miserly doesn't have them. You can undo the last import. Coming from Actual
